UK Brent · M15 · Jan 2025 – Oct 2026

Do Brent spikes give back half within a day?

⚠ True as a description, not yet a trading edge

    1. Does the idea hold up?

    A spike here is a move of at least 3× the recent average bar range within 4 bars (1 hour), measured against the 8 hours before the move. A dip is the same thing downwards. This definition was fixed before looking at any results. The retracement is how far price comes back towards where the move started, as a share of the move, measured over the next 96 bars (one trading day). The test never holds a position over a weekend.

    To tell a real effect from chance, every spike is compared with random moves of the same size starting at random times. In a market with no memory, half of a move still gets retraced most of the time, simply because prices wander, so the comparison has to be against that.

    Trading it: fade the spike, target 50%

    Rules: enter against the spike at the next bar's open. Take profit at the 50% retracement, put the stop 1 ATR beyond the spike's extreme (ATR here is the 8-hour average bar range), and exit after 96 bars if neither is hit. Each trade pays the spread recorded for its bar. Results are in R, multiples of the amount risked on the trade. "Random" places the same target and stop at random times in a random direction, repeated 200 times.

    2. Has this been done before?

    Yes. The idea is a well-studied one. In academic work it is called overreaction and reversal. Traders know it as fading spikes, mean reversion after a volatility shock, or a "liquidity sweep" reversal. The research mostly agrees on two points: reversals after large intraday moves are real and statistically measurable, and once trading costs are counted, very little is left for a trader. The 50% figure has no special standing.

      3. Parameters for an adaptive version

      Four adaptive ways of spotting a spike were tested. Each compares the move with how the market had been behaving just before it, rather than using a fixed number of points:

      • ATR move: the size of the move over N bars, divided by the average bar range of the M bars before it.
      • Z-score: the N-bar return divided by the standard deviation of N-bar returns over the previous M bars.
      • Range: one bar's high-to-low range divided by the median range of the previous M bars.
      • EMA stretch: how far price has moved from an M-bar moving average (EMA), in ATR units.

      Each was swept over window N, context M and threshold k, and combined with every exit setting: retracement target, stop distance, time limit, and entry either immediately or after a confirming bar. The first 9 months (Jan–Oct 2025) were used to choose settings, and the last 12 months to test them.

      Next step

      Don't trade this with real money yet. The one promising group is small, and it was found by searching across both periods. The proper test is to run it forward on new data: an MT5 EA on a demo account, or the Strategy Tester on data after October 2026, with the settings below fixed in advance and every trade journalled. If it keeps around +0.1 R per trade over 150+ new trades, with London/NY-overlap and calm-market trades doing best, then it has earned a small live allocation.
